# Experiment 7: Privacy-Preserving OSINT Exposure Review

## Aim

To assess what information an organization may unintentionally expose through public sources and recommend ways to reduce that exposure.

## Scope and Safety

Use a fictional organization or an organization that has explicitly authorized the review. Do not investigate private individuals, seek non-public information, bypass access controls, contact employees, or probe systems. Keep notes limited to information necessary to describe a risk.

## Materials

- Instructor-provided fictional organization profile, or written authorization and a clearly defined organization scope
- A findings worksheet

## Procedure

1. Write down the approved organization name and scope before beginning. If using a fictional profile, use only the information supplied with it.
2. Review only ordinary, publicly accessible organizational sources that are within scope, such as the organization's own website and public policy or contact pages.
3. Record potential exposures at a high level, such as outdated contact details, overly detailed staff directories, or technology information that is not needed for public use. Do not gather personal details or test whether systems are vulnerable.
4. For each observation, note its source, business impact, confidence, and a privacy-preserving remediation.
5. Remove unnecessary personal information from the report and share it only with the authorized recipient.

## Result

A scoped exposure review was documented without accessing accounts, probing infrastructure, or collecting unnecessary personal information.

## Conclusion

OSINT can help organizations understand their public footprint. Authorization, minimal collection, careful handling, and responsible reporting are essential.
